3D & Visualization

3D Avatars & Vehicle Models

Human-character 3D avatars and car models, including the patient-facing 3D doctor for the healthcare assistant robot.

  • Flutter
  • Three.js
  • Android WebView
  • glTF / GLB
  • Procedural animation

Overview

The 3D doctor replaced the particle orb in the clinic app’s Ask me overlay on the device-selection screen. The standing character uses the existing GLB model in a Three.js scene inside an Android WebView. Flutter manages the assistant session, and the WebView renders the character’s poses, expressions, and mouth movements.

Features

  • A synchronized greeting and wave followed by listening, thinking, and speaking poses
  • English and Hindi lip sync using the model’s Oculus visemes
  • Live half-body and full-body framing with a saved operator setting

Tech stack

  • Flutter
  • Three.js
  • Android WebView
  • glTF / GLB
  • Procedural animation

Patient interaction

The character appears once the patient list is ready and the Ask me overlay opens. The wave starts with “Hello {name}, how are you?” The doctor then listens and speaks the reply with lip sync. If it suggests a checkup, it also speaks the follow-up question. The patient’s yes or no response continues through the existing reports path.

Registration, tests, reports, speech recognition, Gemini speech, and the socket continue to use the existing clinic workflow. View the Assistant Robot case study

Flutter and WebView

Flutter owns the assistant session and sends presentation commands to the WebView. The page stays mounted and moves off-screen when the overlay closes, so reopening it can reuse the loaded doctor.

setSpeaking, setThinking, setListening
Select the assistant pose.
setViseme
Set the mouth shape.
playGesture('hello')
Start the greeting wave.
setHalfBody
Switch the camera framing.

Procedural poses

Each frame starts from the skeleton’s rest pose, which prevents rotations from accumulating. The same GLB preserves the doctor’s face, clothing, body, and identity. Flutter and Three.js handle the animation without an additional animation library.

Only one assistant pose is active at a time. Speaking takes priority over thinking, followed by listening and idle. Switching to listening fades the wave out over about half a second.

Idle
The doctor blinks, breathes subtly, and maintains gentle eye contact with a slight head drift. The hands stay lowered, and a small weight shift runs only when no other motion is active.
Greeting
One arm lifts and waves side to side with gentle wrist motion and a small shoulder lift. A soft head tilt, slight smile, and raised brows accompany the wave. The other hand stays down.
Listening
The hand moves toward the ear and slightly toward the camera to avoid clipping into the head. The doctor tilts toward the patient and occasionally gives a single nod, with a calm expression and the existing listening gaze.
Thinking
The hand rests near the chin with the wrist offset from the face and the fingers relaxed. The brow conveys concentration as the eyes look up and to the side and the head pauses.
Speaking
Outside the greeting, one hand lifts slightly and infrequently while the brows rise. The mouth follows the spoken line. The speaking gesture respects any active wave, ear, or chin gesture.

Lip sync

Flutter maps the spoken text to mouth shapes, including a lightweight Hindi mapping, and fits the sequence to the length of each spoken line. Three.js blends those shapes through the model’s facial morph targets. The model keeps its blink and expression shapes and drops unused face shapes after load to preserve the resting face.

sil, PP, FF, TH, DD, kk, CH, SS, nn, RR, aa, E, I, O, U

Lip sync runs for the greeting, reply, follow-up question, and Hindi replay. The mouth closes at the end of each line.

Camera framing

Half body is the default and frames the head, shoulders, and chest. Full body shows the standing figure. The operator’s “Show half body” switch updates the live camera without reloading the model, and the preference persists across logout.
